# Marine Life — "Did you know?" introduction

Swan, 2026-10-27: the facts go in an introductory section at the front of the
book, not on the animal pages. The animal pages stay like Animals, Volume 1.

Layout plan: **4 intro pages, 13 facts each**, in book order, each fact with
the animal's name in English and Korean. Keep the intro an **even** number of
pages so the three facing pairs (clownfish / sea anemone, hermit crab / turban
shell, sea otter / kelp) stay left and right.

Facts marked ⚑ should be double-checked before printing.

## Draft facts

1. **Shark 상어:** Sharks were swimming in the sea before the dinosaurs! In
   Korea's Gyeongbuk province, salted shark called 돔배기 is set out for
   ancestral rites (제사).
2. **Whale Shark 고래상어:** The biggest fish in the world. ⚑ Its Japanese
   name, ジンベエザメ, comes from the *jinbei*, a summer kimono with a pattern
   like its spots.
3. **Tuna 참치:** Every New Year, Tokyo holds a famous tuna auction. In Korea,
   gift sets of canned tuna are a classic Chuseok present.
4. **Salmon 연어:** Salmon swim all the way back to the river where they
   hatched. Yangyang, Korea, celebrates with a salmon festival every fall.
5. **Mackerel 고등어:** Long ago, mackerel was salted so it would stay fresh on
   the trip inland to Andong. That's how 안동 간고등어 got its name.
6. **Anchovy 멸치:** Tiny but mighty! 멸치 makes Korean soup stock and
   멸치볶음, and in Japan, little dried anchovies (田作り) are a lucky New Year
   food.
7. **Cod 대구:** 대구 means "big mouth" (大口). It sounds just like the city of
   Daegu (大邱), but the characters are different!
8. **Pollock 명태:** Korea's "national fish" has a name for every form: 생태
   fresh, 동태 frozen, 황태 dried in the snow, and 노가리 when it's little. Its
   roe became Japan's *mentaiko* (明太子), named after 명태.
9. **Yellow Croaker 조기:** Dried yellow croaker, 굴비, is a favorite Chuseok
   gift. The most famous comes from Yeonggwang.
10. **Cutlassfish 갈치:** Shaped like a sword and shiny like silver. Jeju Island
    is famous for its silver 은갈치.
11. **Flounder 광어:** A flounder has both eyes on one side of its head! Its
    name means "wide fish," and it's Korea's favorite sashimi.
12. **Pufferfish 복어:** Pufferfish blow up like balloons. In Japan, only
    specially licensed chefs may prepare *fugu*, and in Korea it makes a warm
    soup, 복국.
13. **Sunfish 개복치:** The ocean sunfish can weigh as much as a small car. Its
    reputation as a fragile fish inspired a popular phone game in Japan and
    Korea.
14. **Seahorse 해마:** The dad seahorse carries the babies! And 海馬 is also the
    name of the part of your brain that helps you remember.
15. **Flying Fish 날치:** Flying fish glide above the waves on wing-like fins,
    sometimes farther than a football field. Their tiny orange eggs top Korean
    알밥 and Japanese sushi.
16. **Eel 장어:** Eels swim thousands of kilometers to the deep ocean to lay
    their eggs. In Japan, people eat eel on a special summer day for strength.
17. **Anglerfish 아귀:** It carries its own fishing light on its head! Masan,
    Korea, is famous for spicy 아귀찜.
18. **Stingray 가오리:** The traditional Korean kite 가오리연 is shaped like a
    stingray, long tail and all.
19. **Coral 산호:** Coral looks like a plant, but it's made of tiny animals! Red
    coral was a treasured jewel on traditional hanbok accessories.
20. **Clownfish 흰동가리:** Clownfish live safely among a sea anemone's stinging
    arms. Many kids know it as Nemo, 니모!
21. **Sea Anemone 말미잘:** It looks like a flower, but it's an animal, and its
    sticky arms protect its clownfish friends.
22. **Octopus 문어:** An octopus has three hearts! ⚑ In Korea, 문어 is written 文魚,
    "scholar fish," for its ink, and it's served at weddings and family rites.
23. **Long-arm Octopus 낙지:** ⚑ An old Korean book about sea life, 자산어보
    (1814), says 낙지 can give a tired ox its strength back!
24. **Squid 오징어:** Squid squirt ink to escape. 오징어 is also a Korean
    playground game drawn in the dirt, the one that inspired *Squid Game*.
25. **Cuttlefish 갑오징어:** Cuttlefish can change color in the blink of an eye.
    Its light inner shell, the cuttlebone, is a treat for pet birds.
26. **Jellyfish 해파리:** Jellyfish have no brain and no heart, and they've been
    around for more than 500 million years! In Korea and China, cool jellyfish
    salad is a party dish.
27. **Starfish 불가사리:** If a starfish loses an arm, it can grow a new one!
    불가사리 is also an iron-eating monster in an old Korean folktale.
28. **Sea Urchin 성게:** Jeju's *haenyeo*, women who dive without air tanks,
    gather sea urchins for 성게미역국 soup.
29. **Abalone 전복:** Haenyeo also gather abalone for 전복죽 porridge. Japan's
    gift decoration, *noshi*, began as a strip of dried abalone!
30. **Hermit Crab 소라게:** A hermit crab borrows an empty shell for its home,
    and moves to a bigger one as it grows.
31. **Turban Shell 소라:** Japan's longest-running cartoon, *Sazae-san*, is named
    after this shell (さざえ). And 소라게 means "shell crab."
32. **Blue Crab 꽃게:** Crab marinated in soy sauce, 간장게장, is called a "rice
    thief" (밥도둑) because it makes you eat bowl after bowl!
33. **Snow Crab 대게:** Its long legs look like bamboo (대), so Koreans call it
    대게. Yeongdeok is famous for it.
34. **Shrimp 새우:** In Japan, shrimp are a New Year food for a long life,
    because their bent backs look like an old person's. In Korea, 새우젓 helps
    make kimchi.
35. **Clam 바지락:** 바지락칼국수 is a favorite noodle soup, and in Japan,
    families go clam digging on spring beaches.
36. **Oyster 굴:** Tongyeong in Korea and Hiroshima in Japan are both famous for
    oysters. Some oysters can even make pearls!
37. **Scallop 가리비:** A scallop has up to 200 tiny eyes around its shell, and
    it swims by clapping its shell open and shut.
38. **Nautilus 앵무조개:** A "living fossil": its family has been in the sea for
    about 500 million years. Its Korean name means "parrot shell."
39. **Sea Turtle 바다거북:** In Japan's tale of Urashima Tarō, a sea turtle
    carries a fisherman to the Dragon Palace under the sea. In Korea's
    별주부전, a turtle visits the Dragon King too.
40. **Dolphin 돌고래:** Wild dolphins live off Jeju Island. ⚑ A dolphin named
    제돌이 was famously set free there in 2013.
41. **Orca 범고래:** 범고래 means "tiger whale." The Ainu people of northern Japan
    honored the orca as a god of the sea.
42. **Humpback Whale 혹등고래:** Humpback whales sing long songs. Whales were
    carved thousands of years ago into the Bangudae petroglyphs (반구대
    암각화) in Ulsan, Korea.
43. **Blue Whale 대왕고래:** The biggest animal that has ever lived, bigger than
    any dinosaur! 대왕고래 means "great king whale."
44. **Spotted Seal 점박이물범:** These seals are a Korean Natural Monument and
    spend their summers around Baengnyeongdo Island.
45. **Sea Lion 바다사자:** Sea lions have little ear flaps, and they can walk on
    land by turning their back flippers forward.
46. **Walrus 바다코끼리:** A walrus uses its long tusks to pull itself out of the
    water onto the ice. 바다코끼리 means "sea elephant."
47. **Polar Bear 북극곰:** Under its white fur, a polar bear's skin is black! ⚑ A
    famous shaved ice from Kagoshima, Japan, is called *shirokuma*, "white
    bear," because from above it looks like a polar bear's face.
48. **Sea Otter 해달:** Sea otters hold hands while they sleep so they don't
    drift apart, and wrap themselves in kelp.
49. **Kelp 다시마:** Kelp gives Korean and Japanese soups their flavor. In Japan,
    *kombu* is a lucky food because it sounds like *yorokobu*, "to be happy."
50. **Seagull 갈매기:** Seagulls follow ferries in Korea hoping for 새우깡 snacks,
    and "부산 갈매기" is Busan's famous baseball song.
51. **Wakame 미역:** Koreans eat 미역국 on their birthday. And if someone says they
    "ate 미역국," it can mean they failed an exam!
52. **Nori 김:** Seaweed sheets wrap Korean 김밥 and Japanese onigiri.

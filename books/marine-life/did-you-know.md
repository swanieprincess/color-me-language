# Marine Life — "Did you know?" introduction

Swan, 2026-10-27: the facts go in an introductory section at the front of the
book. The animal pages stay exactly like Animals, Volume 1. Each fact mixes two
things: **what the name means** in Korean, Japanese and Chinese, and **why the
animal matters** in those cultures (food, holidays, folktales, sayings, famous
places). No random nature trivia. Every fact names the animal in all three
languages (Swan, 2026-10-27).

Layout plan: each fact starts with a **small icon of that animal**, a
shrunk-down copy of its coloring-page line art (Swan, 2026-10-27), so kids can
match the fact to the page and color the icons too. Facts in book order, with
the "pieces to spot" key at the top of the first page. With icons and two
sentences per fact, plan on **6 intro pages of about 9 facts each** (4 pages
would be crowded); confirm once the first page is laid out. Keep the intro an **even**
number of pages so the three facing pairs (clownfish / sea anemone, hermit
crab / turban shell, sea otter / kelp) stay left and right.

Facts marked ⚑ should be double-checked before printing.

## Pieces to spot

| Piece | Means | Find it in |
|---|---|---|
| 海 | sea | 海馬 seahorse, 海豚 dolphin, 海星 starfish |
| 魚 | fish | 鯖 mackerel, 鱈 cod, 鯨 whale |
| 虫 | small creature | 蝦 shrimp, 蟹 crab, 螺 shell |
| 鳥 | bird | 鷗 seagull |
| 布 | cloth | 昆布 kelp, 若布 wakame |
| -어 (魚) | fish, at the end of Korean names | 연어, 장어, 광어, 문어 |
| -치 | an old Korean fish ending | 참치, 갈치, 날치, 멸치 |

## The 52

Each heading gives the name in Korean · Japanese · Chinese.

1. **Shark 상어 · さめ · 鯊魚:** Chinese 鯊 is 沙 "sand" on top of 魚 "fish," and
   Japanese writes さめ as 鮫, with 魚 too. In Korea's Gyeongbuk province, salted
   shark called 돔배기 is set out for ancestral rites (제사).
2. **Whale Shark 고래상어 · ジンベエザメ · 鯨鯊:** Korean and Chinese both say
   "whale shark." ⚑ Japanese names it after the *jinbei*, a summer kimono with a
   pattern like its spots. It's the star of Okinawa's Churaumi Aquarium.
3. **Tuna 참치 · まぐろ · 鮪魚:** 참 means "true," and -치 is an old Korean fish
   ending. ⚑ Japanese まぐろ is said to come from 真黒, "true black," for its dark
   back. Japan opens each New Year with a famous tuna auction, and in Korea tuna
   gift sets are a Chuseok classic.
4. **Salmon 연어 · さけ · 鮭魚:** The -어 in 연어 is 魚, "fish," and Japanese and
   Chinese share the character 鮭. Salmon swim home to the river where they
   hatched; Yangyang, Korea, welcomes them with a festival every fall, and Japan
   hangs salted salmon for New Year.
5. **Mackerel 고등어 · さば · 鯖魚:** Japanese and Chinese both write it 鯖: 魚
   "fish" + 青 "blue," for its blue back. Long ago it was salted for the trip
   inland to Andong, which gave Korea 안동 간고등어.
6. **Anchovy 멸치 · かたくちいわし · 鯷魚:** Another -치 fish! Japanese
   かたくちいわし means "one-sided-mouth sardine," and いわし is written 鰯, 魚 +
   弱 "weak." 멸치 makes Korean soup stock, and in Japan dried anchovies (田作り)
   are a lucky New Year food.
7. **Cod 대구 · たら · 鱈魚:** 鱈 is 魚 "fish" + 雪 "snow," a character first made
   in Japan and later used in Chinese too. In Korean, 대구 means "big mouth"
   (大口), and 대구탕 is a favorite winter soup.
8. **Pollock 명태 · すけとうだら · 明太魚:** All three languages share 明太 because
   of the Korean name 명태. Korea's "national fish" has a name for every form:
   생태, 동태, 황태, 노가리. Japan's *mentaiko* (明太子), "pollock's children," is
   made from its eggs.
9. **Yellow Croaker 조기 · キグチ · 黃魚:** Chinese calls it "yellow fish," and ⚑
   Japanese キグチ begins with き, "yellow," too. Dried, it becomes 굴비, one of
   Korea's most treasured Chuseok gifts.
10. **Cutlassfish 갈치 · たちうお · 帶魚:** Three languages, three ideas: Korean 갈치
    comes from 칼 "knife," Japanese 太刀魚 means "sword fish," and Chinese 帶魚
    means "belt fish." Jeju Island is famous for its silver 은갈치.
11. **Flounder 광어 · ひらめ · 比目魚:** Korean says "wide fish" (廣魚), Japanese
    平目 means "flat eyes," and Chinese says "eyes side by side," because both
    eyes are on one side. 광어 is Korea's favorite sashimi.
12. **Pufferfish 복어 · ふぐ · 河豚:** Chinese and Japanese both write it 河豚,
    "river pig," for how round it puffs up. In Japan only licensed chefs may
    prepare *fugu*, and in Korea it makes a warm soup, 복국.
13. **Sunfish 개복치 · マンボウ · 翻車魚:** 개복치 has 복 in it, like 복어, because
    they're cousins. Chinese and Japanese write it 翻車魚, "flipped-cart fish."
    In Korea and Japan it's famous as the "fragile fish" of a popular phone game.
14. **Seahorse 해마 · タツノオトシゴ · 海馬:** Korean and Chinese say "sea horse,"
    but Japanese calls it the "dragon's child" (竜の落とし子)! 海馬 is also the
    name of the part of your brain that helps you remember.
15. **Flying Fish 날치 · トビウオ · 飛魚:** 날 comes from 날다 "to fly," Japanese
    トビ from 飛ぶ "to fly," and Chinese 飛 means "fly." Its tiny orange eggs top
    Korean 알밥 and Japanese sushi.
16. **Eel 장어 · うなぎ · 鰻魚:** 장어 means "long fish" (長魚), and Japanese and
    Chinese share the character 鰻. Japan eats eel on a special summer day for
    strength, and Koreans eat 장어구이 for energy too.
17. **Anglerfish 아귀 · アンコウ · 鮟鱇:** Japanese and Chinese share the
    characters 鮟鱇. ⚑ Korean 아귀 is also a "hungry ghost" from old stories, a
    good name for a fish with such a big mouth. Masan is famous for 아귀찜, and
    Japan for *ankō* hot pot.
18. **Stingray 가오리 · えい · 魟魚:** In Japanese it's simply えい, and Chinese
    writes 魟, with 魚. The traditional Korean kite 가오리연 is named after it,
    with the same diamond shape and long tail, and it's flown at Lunar New Year.
19. **Coral 산호 · さんご · 珊瑚:** Korean 산호 and Japanese さんご both come from
    珊瑚, and both characters have 王, a sign for jade and gems. Red coral was a
    treasured jewel on hanbok accessories like the 노리개.
20. **Clownfish 흰동가리 · クマノミ · 小丑魚:** Chinese says "clown fish," like
    English, and 흰 means "white," for its stripes; Japanese calls it クマノミ.
    Kids around the world know it as Nemo, 니모!
21. **Sea Anemone 말미잘 · いそぎんちゃく · 海葵:** Chinese calls it a "sea
    sunflower," and Japanese いそぎんちゃく means "beach purse," because it
    closes like a drawstring pouch.
22. **Octopus 문어 · たこ · 章魚:** ⚑ Korean 문 (文) and Chinese 章 are both words
    about writing, maybe because of its ink. Japanese writes たこ as 蛸, with 虫.
    In Korea 문어 is served at weddings and family rites, and Japan has takoyaki.
23. **Long-arm Octopus 낙지 · テナガダコ · 長蛸:** Japanese means "long-hand
    octopus" and Chinese "long octopus." ⚑ An old Korean book about sea life,
    자산어보 (1814), says 낙지 can give a tired ox its strength back!
24. **Squid 오징어 · いか · 魷魚:** 오징어 comes from 烏賊, "crow thief," from an old
    story that it plays dead and grabs the crow that pecks it. Japanese writes
    いか with the same characters, 烏賊! And 오징어 is the Korean playground game
    behind *Squid Game*.
25. **Cuttlefish 갑오징어 · こういか · 墨魚:** Korean 갑 and Japanese こう are both
    甲, "armor," for the hard shell inside it, and Chinese 墨魚 means "ink fish."
    Long ago, its ink was used for writing.
26. **Jellyfish 해파리 · くらげ · 水母:** Chinese calls it a "water mother," and
    Japanese くらげ can be written 海月, "sea moon." Cool jellyfish salad is a
    party dish at Korean and Chinese banquets.
27. **Starfish 불가사리 · ひとで · 海星:** Chinese says "sea star," and Japanese
    ひとで means "human hand." 불가사리 is also an iron-eating monster in an old
    Korean folktale!
28. **Sea Urchin 성게 · うに · 海膽:** Chinese 海膽 means "sea gallbladder," and
    Japanese うに is written 雲丹, "red cloud." Jeju's *haenyeo*, women who dive
    without air tanks, gather it for 성게미역국 soup.
29. **Abalone 전복 · あわび · 鮑魚:** Japanese and Chinese share 鮑, which has 魚
    even though abalone isn't a fish. Haenyeo gather it for 전복죽, and Japan's
    gift decoration *noshi* began as a strip of dried abalone.
30. **Hermit Crab 소라게 · ヤドカリ · 寄居蟹:** All three say it borrows a home:
    Korean "shell crab," Japanese "borrowing a room," Chinese "lodger crab."
    Korean kids hunt for them on the beach every summer.
31. **Turban Shell 소라 · さざえ · 蠑螺:** 소라 is inside 소라게, the hermit crab's
    name. Japanese さざえ is the name of Japan's most famous cartoon mom,
    *Sazae-san*, and Chinese 螺 has 虫.
32. **Blue Crab 꽃게 · ガザミ · 梭子蟹:** 꽃게 means "flower crab," Chinese calls it
    the "weaving-shuttle crab," and Japanese calls it ガザミ. In Korea, 간장게장 is
    called a "rice thief" (밥도둑) because you can't stop eating rice with it!
33. **Snow Crab 대게 · ズワイガニ · 雪蟹:** The 대 in 대게 means "bamboo," not "big,"
    and ⚑ Japanese ズワイ means "thin branches": both name its long legs after
    plants. Chinese says "snow crab," like English. Yeongdeok holds a snow crab
    festival every winter.
34. **Shrimp 새우 · えび · 蝦:** Chinese 蝦 has 虫, and Japanese えび is written 海老,
    "old man of the sea," for its bent back, so shrimp are eaten at New Year for
    a long life. In Korea, 새우젓 helps make kimchi.
35. **Clam 바지락 · あさり · 花蛤:** Chinese calls it a "flower clam," and Japanese
    浅蜊 comes from 浅 "shallow," where it lives. Korea loves 바지락칼국수, and
    Japanese families go clam digging on spring beaches.
36. **Oyster 굴 · かき · 牡蠣:** Japanese and Chinese share 牡蠣, and in Korean 굴
    also means "cave." Tongyeong and Hiroshima are both famous for oysters, and
    Koreans mix them into 굴김치.
37. **Scallop 가리비 · ほたて · 扇貝:** Chinese 扇貝 means "fan shell," and Japanese
    帆立 means "raised sail." Grilled scallops are a beach favorite in Korea and
    Hokkaido.
38. **Nautilus 앵무조개 · オウムガイ · 鸚鵡螺:** All three call it a "parrot shell,"
    because its shell looks like a parrot's beak.
39. **Sea Turtle 바다거북 · ウミガメ · 海龜:** "Sea turtle" in all three, and 龜 is
    one of the oldest characters, drawn like a turtle. In Japan's tale of
    Urashima Tarō, a sea turtle carries a fisherman to the Dragon Palace, and in
    Korea's 별주부전 a turtle visits the Dragon King too.
40. **Dolphin 돌고래 · イルカ · 海豚:** Chinese and Japanese both write it 海豚,
    "sea pig." Remember the pufferfish, the "river pig"? Wild dolphins live off
    Jeju Island, and ⚑ one named 제돌이 was famously set free there.
41. **Orca 범고래 · シャチ · 虎鯨:** Korean and Chinese say "tiger whale," and
    Japanese シャチ is written 鯱, 魚 + 虎 "tiger." The Ainu people of northern
    Japan honored the orca as a god of the sea.
42. **Humpback Whale 혹등고래 · ザトウクジラ · 座頭鯨:** 혹 "hump" + 등 "back" +
    고래 "whale," just like English. ⚑ Japanese 座頭 refers to old traveling
    musicians whose lute looked like its curved back. Whales were carved
    thousands of years ago into the Bangudae petroglyphs in Ulsan, Korea.
43. **Blue Whale 대왕고래 · シロナガスクジラ · 藍鯨:** Korean says "great king whale,"
    Chinese says "blue whale," and Japanese says ⚑ "white long whale." 鯨 has 魚
    in it, even though a whale isn't a fish!
44. **Spotted Seal 점박이물범 · ゴマフアザラシ · 斑海豹:** Korean says "spotted water
    tiger," Chinese "spotted sea leopard," and Japanese "sesame-spotted seal."
    These seals are a Korean Natural Monument at Baengnyeongdo Island.
45. **Sea Lion 바다사자 · アシカ · 海獅:** Korean and Chinese say "sea lion," but
    Japanese アシカ is written 海驢, "sea donkey"! Sea lion shows are a favorite
    at aquariums across Korea and Japan.
46. **Walrus 바다코끼리 · セイウチ · 海象:** "Sea elephant" in Korean and Chinese,
    for its long tusks, and Japanese writes セイウチ with the same 海象.
47. **Polar Bear 북극곰 · ホッキョクグマ · 北極熊:** "North Pole bear" in all three
    languages: 북극 is 北極. ⚑ A famous shaved ice from Kagoshima, Japan, is
    called *shirokuma*, "white bear," because it looks like a polar bear's face.
48. **Sea Otter 해달 · ラッコ · 海獺:** 해달 is 海獺, "sea otter," and a river otter
    is 수달, "water otter." Japanese ラッコ comes from the Ainu language, and sea
    otters are beloved stars at Japanese aquariums.
49. **Kelp 다시마 · こんぶ · 昆布:** Japanese and Chinese share 昆布, which has 布
    "cloth," because kelp grows in long ribbons. It flavors Korean and Japanese
    soups, and in Japan *kombu* is lucky because it sounds like *yorokobu*, "to
    be happy."
50. **Seagull 갈매기 · かもめ · 海鷗:** Chinese 鷗 and Japanese 鴎 both have 鳥, the
    piece for birds. Seagulls follow Korean ferries for 새우깡 snacks, and "부산
    갈매기" is Busan's famous baseball song.
51. **Wakame 미역 · わかめ · 裙帶菜:** Chinese calls it "skirt-ribbon vegetable,"
    and Japanese 若布 means "young cloth." Koreans eat 미역국 on every birthday,
    and "I ate 미역국" can mean you failed an exam!
52. **Nori 김 · のり · 紫菜:** Chinese calls it "purple vegetable," and Japanese
    海苔 means "sea moss." ⚑ In Korea, 김 is said to be named after Kim Yeo-ik,
    who first farmed it, and it wraps every 김밥 and Japanese onigiri.

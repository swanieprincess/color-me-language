# Marine Life — "Did you know?" introduction

Swan, 2026-10-27: the facts go in an introductory section at the front of the
book. The animal pages stay exactly like Animals, Volume 1. Each fact mixes two
things: **what the name means** in Korean, Japanese and Chinese, and **why the
animal matters** in those cultures (food, holidays, folktales, sayings, famous
places). No random nature trivia.

Layout plan: each fact starts with a **small icon of that animal**, a
shrunk-down copy of its coloring-page line art (Swan, 2026-10-27), so kids can
match the fact to the page and color the icons too. Facts in book order, with
the "pieces to spot" key at the top of the first page. With icons and two
sentences per fact, plan on **6 intro pages of about 9 facts each** (4 pages
would be crowded); confirm once the first page is laid out. Keep the intro an **even**
number of pages so the three facing pairs (clownfish / sea anemone, hermit
crab / turban shell, sea otter / kelp) stay left and right.

Facts marked ⚑ should be double-checked before printing.

## Pieces to spot

| Piece | Means | Find it in |
|---|---|---|
| 海 | sea | 海馬 seahorse, 海豚 dolphin, 海星 starfish |
| 魚 | fish | 鯖 mackerel, 鱈 cod, 鯨 whale |
| 虫 | small creature | 蝦 shrimp, 蟹 crab, 螺 shell |
| 鳥 | bird | 鷗 seagull |
| 布 | cloth | 昆布 kelp, 若布 wakame |
| -어 (魚) | fish, at the end of Korean names | 연어, 장어, 광어, 문어 |
| -치 | an old Korean fish ending | 참치, 갈치, 날치, 멸치 |

## The 52

1. **Shark 상어 · 鯊:** The character 鯊 is 沙 "sand" on top of 魚 "fish." In
   Korea's Gyeongbuk province, salted shark called 돔배기 is set out for
   ancestral rites (제사).
2. **Whale Shark 고래상어 · 鯨鯊:** Korean and Chinese both say "whale shark,"
   but it's a shark, not a whale! It's the star of Okinawa's Churaumi Aquarium
   in Japan.
3. **Tuna 참치 · 鮪魚:** 참 means "true," and -치 is an old Korean fish ending.
   Japan opens every New Year with a famous tuna auction, and in Korea canned
   tuna gift sets are a classic Chuseok present.
4. **Salmon 연어 · 鮭魚:** The -어 in 연어 is 魚, "fish." Salmon swim home to the
   river where they hatched, and Yangyang, Korea, welcomes them back with a
   salmon festival every fall.
5. **Mackerel 고등어 · 鯖:** 鯖 is 魚 "fish" + 青 "blue," for its blue back. Long
   ago it was salted to stay fresh on the trip inland to Andong, which gave us
   안동 간고등어.
6. **Anchovy 멸치 · 鯷魚:** Another -치 fish! 멸치 makes Korean soup stock and
   멸치볶음, and in Japan little dried anchovies (田作り) are a lucky New Year
   food for a good harvest.
7. **Cod 대구 · 鱈:** 鱈 is 魚 "fish" + 雪 "snow," because cod come in winter. In
   Korean, 대구 means "big mouth" (大口), and 대구탕 is a favorite winter soup.
8. **Pollock 명태 · 明太魚:** Korea's "national fish" has a name for every form:
   생태 fresh, 동태 frozen, 황태 dried in the snow, 노가리 when it's young. Japan's
   *mentaiko* (明太子), "pollock's children," is made from its eggs.
9. **Yellow Croaker 조기 · 黃魚:** Chinese just calls it "yellow fish." Dried, it
   becomes 굴비, one of Korea's most treasured Chuseok gifts.
10. **Cutlassfish 갈치 · 帶魚:** Korean 갈치 comes from 칼 "knife," Japanese
    太刀魚 means "sword fish," and Chinese 帶魚 means "belt fish." Jeju Island is
    famous for its shiny silver 은갈치.
11. **Flounder 광어 · 比目魚:** 광어 means "wide fish," and Chinese 比目魚 means
    "eyes side by side." 광어 is Korea's favorite sashimi.
12. **Pufferfish 복어 · 河豚:** 河豚 means "river pig," for how round it puffs up.
    In Japan, only specially licensed chefs may prepare *fugu*, and in Korea it
    makes a warm soup, 복국.
13. **Sunfish 개복치 · 翻車魚:** 개복치 has 복 in it, like 복어, because they're
    cousins. Chinese calls it the "flipped-cart fish." In Korea and Japan it's
    famous as the "fragile fish" from a popular phone game.
14. **Seahorse 해마 · 海馬:** 海 "sea" + 馬 "horse," in every language. And 海馬
    is also the name of the part of your brain that helps you remember!
15. **Flying Fish 날치 · 飛魚:** 날 comes from 날다 "to fly," and 飛 means "fly."
    Its tiny orange eggs top Korean 알밥 and Japanese sushi.
16. **Eel 장어 · 鰻:** 장어 means "long fish" (長魚). In Japan, people eat eel on a
    special summer day (土用の丑の日) for strength, and Koreans eat 장어구이 for
    energy too.
17. **Anglerfish 아귀 · 鮟鱇:** ⚑ 아귀 is also a "hungry ghost" from old stories, a
    good name for a fish with such a big mouth. Masan is famous for spicy
    아귀찜, and Japan for *ankō* hot pot.
18. **Stingray 가오리 · 魟魚:** The traditional Korean kite 가오리연 is named after
    it, with the same diamond shape and long tail, and it's flown at Lunar New
    Year.
19. **Coral 산호 · 珊瑚:** Both characters have 王, a sign for jade and gems.
    Red coral was a treasured jewel on traditional hanbok accessories like the
    노리개.
20. **Clownfish 흰동가리 · 小丑魚:** Chinese says "clown fish," like English, and
    흰 means "white," for its stripes. Many kids around the world know it as
    Nemo, 니모!
21. **Sea Anemone 말미잘 · 海葵:** Chinese calls it a "sea sunflower." Japanese
    いそぎんちゃく means "beach purse," because it closes like a drawstring
    pouch.
22. **Octopus 문어 · 章魚:** ⚑ Korean 문 (文) and Chinese 章 are both words about
    writing, maybe because of its ink. In Korea, 문어 is served at weddings and
    family rites, and Japan has takoyaki.
23. **Long-arm Octopus 낙지 · 長蛸:** Japanese テナガダコ means "long-hand
    octopus." ⚑ An old Korean book about sea life, 자산어보 (1814), says 낙지 can
    give a tired ox its strength back!
24. **Squid 오징어 · 魷魚:** 오징어 comes from 烏賊, "crow thief," from an old story
    that it plays dead and grabs the crow that pecks it. Japanese writes いか with
    the same characters, and 오징어 is the Korean playground game behind *Squid
    Game*.
25. **Cuttlefish 갑오징어 · 墨魚:** 갑 means "armor," for the hard shell inside it,
    and Chinese 墨魚 means "ink fish." Long ago, its ink was used for writing.
26. **Jellyfish 해파리 · 水母:** Chinese calls it a "water mother," and Japanese
    くらげ can be written 海月, "sea moon." Cool jellyfish salad is a party dish
    at Korean and Chinese banquets.
27. **Starfish 불가사리 · 海星:** Chinese says "sea star," and Japanese ひとで
    means "human hand." 불가사리 is also an iron-eating monster in an old Korean
    folktale!
28. **Sea Urchin 성게 · 海膽:** Japanese うに is written 雲丹, "red cloud." Jeju's
    *haenyeo*, women who dive without air tanks, gather it for 성게미역국 soup.
29. **Abalone 전복 · 鮑魚:** It isn't a fish, but 鮑 has 魚 in it. Haenyeo gather
    it for 전복죽 porridge, and Japan's gift decoration *noshi* began as a strip
    of dried abalone.
30. **Hermit Crab 소라게 · 寄居蟹:** All three languages say it borrows a home:
    Korean "shell crab," Chinese "lodger crab," and Japanese ヤドカリ, "borrowing
    a room." Korean kids hunt for them on the beach every summer.
31. **Turban Shell 소라 · 蠑螺:** 소라 is inside 소라게, the hermit crab's name. In
    Japanese it's さざえ, the name of Japan's most famous cartoon mom,
    *Sazae-san*.
32. **Blue Crab 꽃게 · 梭子蟹:** 꽃게 means "flower crab," and Chinese calls it the
    "weaving-shuttle crab." Crab marinated in soy sauce, 간장게장, is called a
    "rice thief" (밥도둑) because you can't stop eating rice with it!
33. **Snow Crab 대게 · 雪蟹:** The 대 in 대게 means "bamboo," not "big," because its
    legs look like bamboo stalks. Yeongdeok, Korea, holds a snow crab festival
    every winter.
34. **Shrimp 새우 · 蝦:** Japanese えび is written 海老, "old man of the sea," for
    its bent back, so shrimp are eaten at New Year for a long life. In Korea,
    새우젓 helps make kimchi.
35. **Clam 바지락 · 花蛤:** Chinese calls it a "flower clam" for its patterned
    shell. Korea loves 바지락칼국수 noodle soup, and Japanese families go clam
    digging on spring beaches.
36. **Oyster 굴 · 牡蠣:** In Korean, 굴 also means "cave." Tongyeong in Korea and
    Hiroshima in Japan are both famous for oysters, and Koreans mix them into
    굴김치.
37. **Scallop 가리비 · 扇貝:** 扇貝 means "fan shell," and Japanese ほたて (帆立)
    means "raised sail." Grilled scallops are a beach favorite in Korea and
    Hokkaido.
38. **Nautilus 앵무조개 · 鸚鵡螺:** All three languages call it a "parrot shell,"
    because its shell looks like a parrot's beak. Its spiral shell has been
    treasured as art for centuries.
39. **Sea Turtle 바다거북 · 海龜:** "Sea turtle" in every language, and 龜 is one
    of the oldest characters, drawn like a turtle. In Japan's tale of Urashima
    Tarō, a sea turtle carries a fisherman to the Dragon Palace, and in Korea's
    별주부전 a turtle visits the Dragon King too.
40. **Dolphin 돌고래 · 海豚:** In Chinese, and in the kanji for Japanese イルカ, a
    dolphin is 海豚, a "sea pig." Wild dolphins live off Jeju Island, and ⚑ one
    named 제돌이 was famously set free there.
41. **Orca 범고래 · 虎鯨:** Korean and Chinese both say "tiger whale," and Japanese
    シャチ is written 鯱, 魚 + 虎. The Ainu people of northern Japan honored the
    orca as a god of the sea.
42. **Humpback Whale 혹등고래 · 座頭鯨:** 혹 "hump" + 등 "back" + 고래 "whale,"
    just like English. Whales were carved thousands of years ago into the
    Bangudae petroglyphs (반구대 암각화) in Ulsan, Korea.
43. **Blue Whale 대왕고래 · 藍鯨:** 대왕 means "great king," and 鯨 has 魚 in it,
    even though a whale isn't a fish. It's the biggest animal that has ever
    lived.
44. **Spotted Seal 점박이물범 · 斑海豹:** 점박이 means "spotted." Korean calls a seal
    a "water tiger" (물범), and Chinese a "sea leopard" (海豹). These seals are a
    Korean Natural Monument and spend summers at Baengnyeongdo Island.
45. **Sea Lion 바다사자 · 海獅:** "Sea lion" in every language: 바다 + 사자, 海 +
    獅. Sea lion shows are a favorite at aquariums across Korea and Japan.
46. **Walrus 바다코끼리 · 海象:** Korean and Chinese both call it a "sea elephant,"
    for its long tusks.
47. **Polar Bear 북극곰 · 北極熊:** 북극 is 北極, "North Pole," so it's the "North
    Pole bear" in all three languages. ⚑ A famous shaved ice from Kagoshima,
    Japan, is called *shirokuma*, "white bear," because it looks like a polar
    bear's face.
48. **Sea Otter 해달 · 海獺:** 해달 is 海獺, "sea otter," and a river otter is
    수달, "water otter." Sea otters are beloved stars at Japanese aquariums.
49. **Kelp 다시마 · 昆布:** 昆布 has 布, "cloth," because kelp grows in long
    ribbons. It flavors Korean and Japanese soups, and in Japan *kombu* is lucky
    because it sounds like *yorokobu*, "to be happy."
50. **Seagull 갈매기 · 海鷗:** 鷗 has 鳥, the piece for birds. Seagulls follow
    Korean ferries for 새우깡 snacks, and "부산 갈매기" is Busan's famous baseball
    song.
51. **Wakame 미역 · 裙帶菜:** Chinese calls it "skirt-ribbon vegetable," and
    Japanese 若布 means "young cloth." Koreans eat 미역국 on every birthday, and
    "I ate 미역국" can mean you failed an exam!
52. **Nori 김 · 紫菜:** Chinese calls it "purple vegetable" for its dark color. ⚑ In
    Korea, 김 is said to be named after Kim Yeo-ik, who first farmed it, and it
    wraps every 김밥.

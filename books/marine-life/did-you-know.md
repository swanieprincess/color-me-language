# Marine Life — "Did you know?" introduction

Swan, 2026-10-27: the facts go in an introductory section at the front of the
book. The animal pages stay exactly like Animals, Volume 1. Each fact mixes two
things: **what the name means** in Korean, Japanese and Chinese, and **why the
animal matters** in those cultures (food, holidays, folktales, sayings, famous
places). No random nature trivia. Every fact names the animal in all three
languages (Swan, 2026-10-27).

Layout plan: each fact starts with a **small icon of that animal**, a
shrunk-down copy of its coloring-page line art (Swan, 2026-10-27), so kids can
match the fact to the page and color the icons too. Facts in book order, with
the "pieces to spot" key at the top of the first page. With icons and two
sentences per fact, plan on **6 intro pages of about 9 facts each** (4 pages
would be crowded); confirm once the first page is laid out. Keep the intro an **even**
number of pages so the three facing pairs (clownfish / sea anemone, hermit
crab / turban shell, sea otter / kelp) stay left and right.

Facts marked ⚑ should be double-checked before printing.

## Pieces to spot

| Piece | Means | Find it in |
|---|---|---|
| 海 | sea | 海馬 seahorse, 海豚 dolphin, 海星 starfish |
| 魚 | fish | 鯖 mackerel, 鱈 cod, 鯨 whale |
| 虫 | small creature | 蝦 shrimp, 蟹 crab, 螺 shell |
| 鳥 | bird | 鷗 seagull |
| 布 | cloth | 昆布 kelp, 若布 wakame |
| -어 (魚) | fish, at the end of Korean names | 연어, 장어, 광어, 문어 |
| -치 | an old Korean fish ending | 참치, 갈치, 날치, 멸치 |

## The 52

Written for kids ages 3–12: short sentences and simple words, easy for a
parent to read aloud. Each heading gives the name in Korean · Japanese ·
Chinese.

1. **Shark 상어 · さめ · 鯊魚:** In Chinese, shark is 鯊: "sand" (沙) on top of
   "fish" (魚). In parts of Korea, salted shark called 돔배기 is a special food
   families set out on holidays to honor their grandparents and ancestors (제사).
2. **Whale Shark 고래상어 · ジンベエザメ · 鯨鯊:** Korean and Chinese call it a
   "whale shark." ⚑ Its Japanese name comes from a summer outfit with spots just
   like its skin! You can visit one at a big aquarium in Okinawa, Japan.
3. **Tuna 참치 · まぐろ · 鮪魚:** 참 means "true," and -치 is an old Korean ending
   for fish. ⚑ Japanese まぐろ may come from "true black," for its dark back.
   Tuna is a favorite in Japanese sushi and Korean 참치김밥.
4. **Salmon 연어 · さけ · 鮭魚:** The -어 in 연어 means "fish." Japanese and Chinese
   use the same character, 鮭. Salmon swim all the way home to the river where
   they were born, and a town in Korea throws them a festival every fall!
5. **Mackerel 고등어 · さば · 鯖魚:** Japanese and Chinese write it 鯖: "fish" (魚)
   + "blue" (青), for its shiny blue back. Grilled 고등어 is a favorite family
   dinner in Korea.
6. **Anchovy 멸치 · かたくちいわし · 鯷魚:** Another -치 fish! Japanese
   かたくちいわし means "one-sided-mouth sardine." These tiny fish make Korean
   soup tasty and become crunchy 멸치볶음 in lunch boxes.
7. **Cod 대구 · たら · 鱈魚:** 鱈 is "fish" (魚) + "snow" (雪), because cod come in
   snowy winter. In Korean, 대구 means "big mouth"! Warm 대구탕 soup is a winter
   favorite.
8. **Pollock 명태 · すけとうだら · 明太魚:** All three languages use 明太 because of
   the Korean name 명태. It's Korea's favorite fish, with a different name when
   it's fresh, frozen, dried or little: 생태, 동태, 황태, 노가리!
9. **Hammerhead Shark 귀상어 · シュモクザメ · 雙髻鯊:** Korean says "ear shark,"
   because its head looks like two big ears. ⚑ Japanese シュモク is the T-shaped
   wooden hammer that rings a temple bell, and Chinese says "two-bun shark,"
   like hair tied up in two buns!
10. **Cutlassfish 갈치 · たちうお · 帶魚:** Three languages, three ideas! Korean 갈치
    comes from 칼, "knife." Japanese says "sword fish," and Chinese says "belt
    fish." It's long, thin and shiny like silver.
11. **Flounder 광어 · ひらめ · 比目魚:** Korean says "wide fish," Japanese says "flat
    eyes," and Chinese says "eyes side by side." That's because both of its eyes
    are on the same side of its head!
12. **Pufferfish 복어 · ふぐ · 河豚:** Japanese and Chinese both write it 河豚,
    "river pig," because it puffs up round like a little pig. In Japan, only
    specially trained chefs are allowed to cook it.
13. **Sunfish 개복치 · マンボウ · 翻車魚:** 개복치 has 복 in it, like 복어, because
    they're cousins! Japanese and Chinese write it 翻車魚, "flipped-cart fish."
14. **Seahorse 해마 · タツノオトシゴ · 海馬:** Korean and Chinese say "sea horse,"
    just like English. Japanese calls it the "dragon's baby"! 해마 is also the
    name of the part of your brain that helps you remember.
15. **Flying Fish 날치 · トビウオ · 飛魚:** Korean 날, Japanese トビ and Chinese 飛
    all mean "fly"! Its tiny orange eggs are a colorful topping on Korean rice
    bowls and Japanese sushi.
16. **Eel 장어 · うなぎ · 鰻魚:** 장어 means "long fish." Japanese and Chinese use
    the same character, 鰻. In Japan and Korea, people eat eel in summer to feel
    strong.
17. **Anglerfish 아귀 · アンコウ · 鮟鱇:** Japanese and Chinese share the same
    characters, 鮟鱇. ⚑ In old Korean stories, 아귀 is a creature that's always
    hungry, a perfect name for a fish with such a giant mouth!
18. **Stingray 가오리 · えい · 魟魚:** In Japanese it's simply えい. The Korean kite
    가오리연 is named after the stingray, with the same diamond shape and long
    tail. Families fly it at Lunar New Year!
19. **Coral 산호 · さんご · 珊瑚:** Korean 산호 and Japanese さんご both come from
    珊瑚. Both characters have 王, a sign for treasure, because red coral was
    made into beautiful jewelry for hanbok.
20. **Clownfish 흰동가리 · クマノミ · 小丑魚:** Chinese says "clown fish," just like
    English! 흰 means "white," for its white stripes, and in Japanese it's
    クマノミ. Lots of kids know it as Nemo, 니모!
21. **Sea Anemone 말미잘 · いそぎんちゃく · 海葵:** It looks like a flower, but it's
    an animal! Chinese calls it a "sea sunflower," and Japanese calls it a "beach
    purse," because it closes up like a little bag.
22. **Octopus 문어 · たこ · 章魚:** ⚑ Korean 문 and Chinese 章 are both words about
    writing, maybe because octopuses squirt ink! Japanese kids love takoyaki,
    little round octopus snacks.
23. **Long-arm Octopus 낙지 · テナガダコ · 長蛸:** Japanese says "long-hand octopus,"
    and Chinese says "long octopus." ⚑ An old Korean book says 낙지 can help a
    tired cow feel strong again!
24. **Squid 오징어 · いか · 魷魚:** 오징어 comes from the characters 烏賊, "crow
    thief." An old story says it floats like it's asleep, then grabs a crow!
    Japanese writes いか with the same characters.
25. **Cuttlefish 갑오징어 · こういか · 墨魚:** Korean 갑 and Japanese こう both mean
    "armor," for the hard shell inside it. Chinese calls it "ink fish," and long
    ago its ink was used for writing.
26. **Jellyfish 해파리 · くらげ · 水母:** Chinese calls it a "water mother," and
    Japanese can write くらげ as "sea moon" (海月). Isn't that pretty?
27. **Starfish 불가사리 · ひとで · 海星:** Chinese says "sea star," and Japanese says
    "human hand," because it has five arms like fingers. In an old Korean story,
    불가사리 is also a monster that eats iron!
28. **Sea Urchin 성게 · うに · 海膽:** Japanese writes うに as "red cloud" (雲丹),
    for its orange-red inside. On Jeju Island, women divers called *haenyeo*
    gather sea urchins for a special seaweed soup.
29. **Abalone 전복 · あわび · 鮑魚:** Japanese and Chinese write it 鮑, with "fish"
    (魚) in it, even though it's a shellfish! Jeju's *haenyeo* divers gather
    abalone for 전복죽, a cozy rice porridge.
30. **Hermit Crab 소라게 · ヤドカリ · 寄居蟹:** All three languages say it borrows a
    home! Korean says "shell crab," Japanese says "borrowing a room," and Chinese
    says "guest crab." Korean kids love finding them at the beach.
31. **Turban Shell 소라 · さざえ · 蠑螺:** 소라 is inside 소라게, the hermit crab's
    name! In Japan, さざえ is also the name of a famous cartoon mom, Sazae-san.
32. **Blue Crab 꽃게 · ガザミ · 梭子蟹:** 꽃게 means "flower crab," and in Japanese
    it's ガザミ. Chinese names it after a pointy weaving tool. In Korea, crab in
    soy sauce is called a "rice thief" because it makes you want more rice!
33. **Snow Crab 대게 · ズワイガニ · 雪蟹:** The 대 in 대게 means "bamboo," because
    its long legs look like bamboo! ⚑ Japanese ズワイ means "thin branches."
    Chinese says "snow crab," just like English.
34. **Shrimp 새우 · えび · 蝦:** Japanese writes えび as "old man of the sea" (海老),
    because a shrimp's back is bent like a grandpa's. That's why it's eaten at
    New Year for a long life! In Korea, tiny salted shrimp help make kimchi.
35. **Clam 바지락 · あさり · 花蛤:** Chinese calls it a "flower clam" for its pretty
    shell, and Japanese あさり comes from "shallow," where it lives. In Japan,
    families dig for clams at the beach in spring.
36. **Oyster 굴 · かき · 牡蠣:** Japanese and Chinese use the same characters, 牡蠣.
    In Korean, 굴 also means "cave"! Some oysters can even make pearls.
37. **Scallop 가리비 · ほたて · 扇貝:** Chinese says "fan shell," because it's
    shaped like a folding fan. Japanese ほたて means "raised sail."
38. **Nautilus 앵무조개 · オウムガイ · 鸚鵡螺:** All three languages call it a
    "parrot shell," because its shell looks like a parrot's beak!
39. **Sea Turtle 바다거북 · ウミガメ · 海龜:** "Sea turtle" in all three languages.
    In a Japanese folktale, a sea turtle carries a fisherman to a palace under
    the sea, and in the Korean tale 별주부전, a turtle visits the Dragon King too!
40. **Dolphin 돌고래 · イルカ · 海豚:** Japanese and Chinese write it 海豚, "sea
    pig." Remember the pufferfish, the "river pig"? Wild dolphins swim near Jeju
    Island in Korea.
41. **Orca 범고래 · シャチ · 虎鯨:** Korean and Chinese call it a "tiger whale," and
    Japanese writes シャチ as "fish" + "tiger" (鯱). The Ainu people of northern
    Japan honored the orca as a god of the sea.
42. **Humpback Whale 혹등고래 · ザトウクジラ · 座頭鯨:** 혹 "hump" + 등 "back" + 고래
    "whale," just like English! Long, long ago, people in Ulsan, Korea, carved
    pictures of whales into the rocks, and you can still see them today.
43. **Blue Whale 대왕고래 · シロナガスクジラ · 藍鯨:** Korean says "great king whale,"
    Chinese says "blue whale," and ⚑ Japanese says "white long whale." It's the
    biggest animal that has ever lived, even bigger than the dinosaurs!
44. **Spotted Seal 점박이물범 · ゴマフアザラシ · 斑海豹:** Korean says "spotted water
    tiger," Chinese says "spotted sea leopard," and Japanese says "sesame-spotted
    seal." Korea protects these seals as a national treasure.
45. **Sea Lion 바다사자 · アシカ · 海獅:** Korean and Chinese say "sea lion," but
    Japanese writes アシカ as "sea donkey" (海驢)! Sea lion shows are a favorite
    at aquariums in Korea and Japan.
46. **Walrus 바다코끼리 · セイウチ · 海象:** Korean, Japanese and Chinese all call it
    a "sea elephant," because its long tusks look like an elephant's!
47. **Polar Bear 북극곰 · ホッキョクグマ · 北極熊:** "North Pole bear" in all three
    languages! ⚑ In Japan, a shaved ice treat called *shirokuma*, "white bear,"
    looks like a polar bear's face.
48. **Sea Otter 해달 · ラッコ · 海獺:** 해달 means "sea otter." Its cousin, the river
    otter, is 수달, "water otter." Japanese ラッコ comes from the language of the
    Ainu people.
49. **Kelp 다시마 · こんぶ · 昆布:** Japanese and Chinese write it 昆布, with 布,
    "cloth," because kelp grows in long ribbons. In Japan, こんぶ is a lucky
    food because it sounds like the word for "happy."
50. **Seagull 갈매기 · かもめ · 海鷗:** Chinese 鷗 and Japanese 鴎 both have 鳥, the
    sign for "bird." In Korea, seagulls follow ferry boats hoping for shrimp
    crackers called 새우깡!
51. **Wakame 미역 · わかめ · 裙帶菜:** Chinese calls it "skirt-ribbon vegetable," and
    Japanese わかめ means "young cloth." In Korea, everyone eats 미역국, seaweed
    soup, on their birthday!
52. **Nori 김 · のり · 紫菜:** Chinese calls it "purple vegetable," and Japanese
    のり means "sea moss." It wraps Korean 김밥 and Japanese rice balls,
    called onigiri.
